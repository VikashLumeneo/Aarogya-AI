The Problem
Handwritten prescriptions cause preventable harm

Illegible handwriting, missing information, and dangerous abbreviations lead to prescription errors that affect patient safety and create legal risk for physicians.

Illegible handwriting
Drug names, doses, and instructions misread by pharmacists — resulting in wrong medications or wrong doses dispensed.
Missing critical information
Prescriptions often lack patient weight, known allergies, route of administration, or follow-up instructions — each a potential safety gap.
Dangerous abbreviations
Latin abbreviations and unsafe symbols ('U' misread as '0' or '4', 'µg' misread as 'mg') can result in 1,000-fold overdoses. MCI guidelines prohibit them.
Undetected drug interactions
Without access to a patient's full medication history at the point of prescribing, interactions go undetected until harm occurs.
The Arogya Solution
AI Rx eliminates every one of these risks

Generated from the clinical encounter, AI Rx produces structured, complete, and legible prescriptions — every time.

Always legible, always structured
Digital generation means zero handwriting errors. Medicines in capital letters with correct spacing between name and strength, exactly as MCI requires.
Complete by default
Every Rx includes patient name, age, sex, weight, address, allergy status, diagnosis, dose, route, frequency, duration, and follow-up — nothing is left blank.
Local language instructions, no Latin abbreviations
Medication instructions use plain language directions — "Three times a day", "After food" — never error-prone Latin abbreviations. An abbreviation key is included for reference.
Drug interaction check before it prints
Our AI cross-references newly prescribed medications against the patient's known medication history and flags any interactions for physician review before the Rx is finalized.
Generic names only, per EML
Prescriptions use generic (INN) drug names as required by MCI guidelines and the Essential Medicines List, reducing cost for patients and ambiguity for pharmacists.
